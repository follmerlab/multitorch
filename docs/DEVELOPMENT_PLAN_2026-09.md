So the answer to "where did we end up on going beyond Oh and D4h" is: **nowhere yet, and D4h itself is only complete for even-electron ions.** The `fits/` Fe work still runs in the Oh approximation for exactly that reason (Fe(III) d5 is half-integer J). The 714 eV residual in every v0 fit is the symptom.

### What the Fortran suite could do that we cannot yet

`ttrac` reads Butler's branching library (`ttmult/inputs/disk0`), which tabulates the point groups **O, T, K, C1–C6, D2–D6, D∞** (inversion handled by parity labels, so Oh = O×Ci, D4h = D4×Ci, Td ≅ O with relabeling). pyctm only ever wrote chains for `oh`, `d4h`, `c4h`. CTM4XAS users expect at least Oh, Td, D4h, C4v, D3d, D2h, C2v.

18. **Unnormalised Boltzmann weights put a kink in the spectrum at every ground-level crossing (found 2026-09-13 during A5; kept by decision D-1, see "Deferred decisions" below).** `get_sticks*` weights by `exp((E_min − E_g)/kT)` without dividing by the partition function, exactly as pyctm (`pyctm/get_spectrum.py:84`). The total weight Z·exp(E_min/kT) is then non-differentiable wherever the lowest ground branch switches, e.g. the D4h Oh limit dt = 0: from scratch, Ni²⁺ at T = 80 K, max_gs = 40, the weighted loss has one-sided slopes +2.41 / −5.55 (normalised to L) and its area peaks at dt = 0. Autograd returns one side, central FD their mean (−1.57); neither is "the" derivative. Dividing by the pool partition function makes both slopes −1.574. **Consequence:** the planned A5 oracle (FD at dt = ds = 0 and at soc = 0) cannot pass on the current forward model; with max_gs = 1 the same points are also discontinuous (residual 15, FD ≈ ±5000). 19. **The A5 design as written would be wrong at exact degeneracy (found 2026-09-13).** For a loss tr(f(H)A) (Boltzmann-weighted intensities are of this form) the exact gradient has Daleckii–Krein divided differences (f(λ_i) − f(λ_j))/(λ_i − λ_j) → f′(λ) on degenerate pairs. `eigh` backward only sees (Uᵀ∂U)_ij, whose antisymmetric part carries f(λ_i) − f(λ_j) = 0 at exact degeneracy, so a Lorentzian-regularised 1/(λ_i − λ_j) drops the f′·A_ij term (basis-dependent result whenever A is not ∝ I on the degenerate space, e.g. accidental degeneracies of copies inside one irrep block, or ζ = 0). The existing 1e-12·linspace diagonal perturbation splits the pair by δ and so recovers f′ to O(δ) — it is closer to right than the Lorentzian. Its real defect is that it shifts the *forward* eigenvalues only when `requires_grad`, so grad and no-grad evaluations can count ground levels differently under residual 15. Recommended A5 instead: exact forward (no shift) + backward that perturbs only inside the backward, or wait for residual 18/15 decisions.

21. **From-scratch D4h depended on the machine's LAPACK (fixed 2026-09-14).** `_oh_irrep_matrices_real_std` took the Oh E-irrep partner basis from `np.linalg.eigh` of a projector whose eigenvalue-1 space is two-dimensional. Every rotation there is a valid Oh basis, but the partner-resolved Oh→D4h subduction needs partner 0 ∝ d(x²−y²) and partner 1 ∝ d(z²) *with equal signs*; macOS Accelerate happened to return an admissible basis, exxa's LAPACK did not. Oracles: Ni D4h from scratch vs the Fortran store at two (10Dq, Dt, Ds) points (≤ 2e-5 eV), and the same with eigenvectors randomly rotated inside every degenerate subspace in a fresh process (`tests/tools/lapack_scramble.py`; seed 2 reproduced exxa's wrong levels exactly before the fix). Fe²⁺/V³⁺ Oh and D4h spectra are invariant to the scrambling to ≤ 2e-12. **Hidden sign convention fixed 2026-09-15.** The D4h Dt operator is the Butler-weighted sum of two routes (Oh A1 and Oh E at rank 4), each built from eigen-decompositions with arbitrary sign; the Ballhausen pin fixed only the sign of the sum, so their *relative* sign was LAPACK's choice. Flipping eigenvector signs (which LAPACK builds also do differently, not only rotations in degenerate spaces) broke Dt by up to 1.6 eV. Each route is now pinned to a positive m = 0 real-harmonic component, the convention the Butler coefficients were calibrated to; with it an E-partner sign flip is harmless. The scramble harness now also flips every eigen/singular vector sign and is deterministic per input matrix (a non-deterministic scramble falsely broke uncached, repeated calls). Oracle: Ni D4h vs Fortran at four (10Dq, Dt, Ds) points incl. Ds-only and Dt-only, unscrambled and for two scramble seeds; without the route pin seed 2 fails at 0.66 eV. WP-B's general `PointGroup` must gauge-fix every operator vector the same way. 29. **Half-integer RAC coefficients can be real (2026-09-17).** The double-group irreps are pseudo-real, so the projection gives complex partner vectors while a RAC coefficient must be a single real number; it was not obvious that a gauge exists making all of them real at once, and the alternative (complex Hermitian blocks in the assembler) would have been invasive. 40. **The trigonal point groups were registered with C3 along z, which is not the setting a distorted octahedron needs (found 2026-10-19 by WP-B3; CLOSED 2026-10-20 by WP-B5-8).** `finite_group.GENERATORS` puts every principal axis along z, which is the crystallographic standard and is right for the tetragonal and orthorhombic groups: D4h, D4, C4v, D2d, D2h, D2 and C2v are all genuine subgroups of the standard Oh in that setting, and the cubic invariant is one of their invariants to <= 6.5e-16, which is what makes "10Dq plus a distortion" a meaningful parameterisation. **The trigonal groups are not.** The trigonal subgroup of an octahedron has its C3 along a body diagonal, so with C3 along z, **8 of D3d's 12 elements and 4 of C3v's 6 lie outside Oh**, and the cubic invariant is correspondingly *not* in their rank-4 invariant space: residual **0.6455**, identical for D3d, D3 and C3v since they share the axis. It is not. `_d4_double_irrep_matrices` does not construct D4's double irreps at all -- it takes them whole, as restrictions of Oh's: E1/2 is Gamma6 restricted to the 8 D4_z rotations and E3/2 is Gamma7 restricted, both irreducible there, "the cheapest correct realization". **Measured for D3 and that shortcut is unavailable.** D3's double group has **three** spinor irreps, `1E3/2` and `2E3/2` of dimension **one** and `E1/2` of dimension two; Gamma6 and Gamma7 both restrict to irreducible 2-D reps that are *equivalent to each other* (so they give one irrep, not two), and the two 1-D irreps occur nowhere except inside Gamma8's restriction, which splits `[2, 1, 1]`. Every D4h double irrep is 2-D, so the half-integer path has never seen dimension 1. **Butler's table in the tree says exactly this and settles the labelling.** `ttmult/src/disk0`, block `$F3/OD3`, header "BRANCHDATA O=>D3 CREATED FOR_BUTLER(1981) 28_MAR_1980": `BRANCHINGS 0 +0 ; S0 +S0 ; 1 +^0 +1 ; S1 +S0 +S1 +-S1 ; 2 +1 ; ^1 +0 +1 ; ^S0 +S0 ; ^0 +^0 ;`. Read with the O labels `derive_butler_routes.py` already uses, that is `T1 -> A2 + E`, `T2 -> A1 + E`, `E -> E` -- confirming residual 59's single-group descent against the table ttrac itself reads rather than against `point_group` -- and `Gamma8 -> E1/2 + S1 + (-S1)`, naming the two 1-D spinor irreps as the complex-conjugate pair they are. **What landed:** the table transcribed with provenance, a composition oracle (SO3 -> Oh branching followed by Butler's table reproduces `point_group('D3', spinor=True).branching` for J = 0 .. 11/2, integer and half-integer, all twelve, by two routes neither derived from the other), the restriction split asserted with an irreducibility check per block, a drift guard that re-reads the `BRANCHINGS` line from `disk0` when the Fortran tree is reachable, and an injection that swaps T2's row for T1's and requires J = 2 to notice. **What did not land, and why:** the D3d double subduction itself. The construction is clear -- take each target double irrep from a fixed Oh source, whole where the restriction is irreducible (which preserves D4h bit for bit) and by isotypic extraction from Gamma8 otherwise -- but the choice of which 1-D block is `S1` and which is `-S1` is a free convention, it must be consistent across every J, and `target_double_irrep_dim`, the layout and the Kramers handling all currently assume dimension 2 on the half-integer side. **The lesson, and it is the sharpest one this session.** The first commutant solve used the column-major form of the vec identity, which is the natural thing to write and is wrong for numpy's row-major `reshape`. It returned matrices commuting with **nothing** -- max |XD - DX| of 0.7 -- and nevertheless split Gamma8 as `[1, 1, 2]`, which is *exactly* what Butler says, with both 1-D blocks reported at the right dimension. Had the irreducibility norm not come out at 0.333 instead of 1.0 it would have been taken as confirmation. **A decomposition can land on the right block structure out of arithmetic that does not commute; the invariance is the thing to assert, not the shapes.** The test's helper now asserts commutation before using the basis, and says in its docstring what it is guarding against.

- **B1. `PointGroup` object — DONE (2026-10-18, `angular/finite_group.py`).** Built from generators as designed: quaternion elements (so the SU(2) double cover is free — `PointGroup(..., spinor=True)` keeps `±q` apart and `spinor=False` identifies them), improper operations as an explicit `parity` flag rather than a `'g'`/`'u'` suffix, closure with a verified group axiom check on construction, numerical conjugacy classes, and the character table by the **class-sum (Dixon) method** — no irrep matrices needed: the class multiplication matrices commute, their simultaneous eigenvalues are the central characters λ_i^μ = |C_i|χ^μ(g_i)/d_μ, and d_μ = √(|G|/Σ_i|λ_i^μ|²/|C_i|). Mulliken labels are derived from dimension plus character signature, with the family (cubic / axial / biaxial) found from how many C3 axes and proper C2 axes the group has, and the **double group's extra irreps named for the lowest half-integer J that contains them**, which reproduces the package's own `E1/2`, `E5/2`, `G3/2` and D4h's `E1/2`, `E3/2` without being told them. 22 groups registered; the 13 WP-B targets (Oh, O, Td, D4h, D4, C4v, D3d, D3, C3v, D2h, D2, C2v, D2d) all come out with conventional labels in both the single and double group, and `labels_are_conventional` is `False` — not faked — for the complex-conjugate-pair families C3, C4, C4h, T, Th, whose Mulliken primes are not implemented.

  Oracles (153 tests, 1.5 s, `tests/test_angular/test_finite_group.py`): **Oh branching equals `point_group.oh_branching` for J = 0..12**, two constructions sharing no code, the latter Fortran-validated; the 24 elements of O equal `octahedral_rotations()`; the Oh double group reproduces the standard Koster reduction of D^(J) for J = 1/2..11/2 (Γ6, Γ8, Γ7⊕Γ8, Γ6⊕Γ7⊕Γ8, Γ6⊕2Γ8, Γ6⊕Γ7⊕2Γ8) and the package's `OH_DOUBLE_IRREP_DIM` / `D4H_IRREP_DIM` label sets exactly; textbook orders and class counts for 22 groups; both character orthogonality relations; and the isomorphisms O≅Td, D4≅C4v≅D2d, D3≅C3v, D2≅C2v, which catch a table that is self-consistent but belongs to the wrong group.

  **One real defect was found by insisting the injected-bug test actually fail** (and is worth remembering for B2–B5): orthonormality and Burnside are *not* sufficient to validate a character table. Corrupting a_{1,1,1} or a_{4,4,4} of D4h's class algebra yields a table wrong in **every row** that passes both orthogonality relations and Σd² = |G|. The guard that closes it asks whether the rows are characters *of this group* at all — every tensor product must decompose into non-negative integers, m_{μν}^λ = (1/|G|)Σ_i|C_i|χ^μχ^ν χ̄^λ — which depends only on the class sizes (from the multiplication table) and so is independent of the coefficients under test. All eight injected corruption sites are now refused; the test parametrises over them.

  A second, smaller trap, recorded because anything reading an element's rotation angle will hit it: **inversion must not be written as a 2π rotation.** `quaternion(z, 2π)` has w = −1 — the same O(3) operation, but `Element.angle` then reports 2π, and the first version of the Mulliken labeller lost every single `g`/`u` suffix because its probes read that angle. There is an explicit `INVERSION` element, and the geometric probes fold the angle to [0, π] so they cannot see the spinor sign.

- **B2. Direct O(3)→G subduction — DONE (2026-10-18, `angular/finite_group.py`).** `PointGroup.subduction(J, parity)` → `(S, labels)` with `labels[k] = (irrep, copy, partner)`, by row projection `P[μ,ν] = (d/|G|) Σ_g conj(Γ(g)[μ,ν]) D(g)`. Two ingredients were needed beyond B1:

  **(i) Numerical irrep *matrices*, not just characters** (`PointGroup.irrep_matrices`). Characters cannot split an isotypic subspace into partners. Extracted with no tabulated data from the fact that the left and right regular representations commute: the Γ-isotypic subspace of the left regular rep is Γ ⊗ C^d with the left action on the first factor and the commutant on the second; the commutant is spanned by the *right* regular rep restricted there, free of charge; a generic Hermitian element of it has d eigenvalues with d-dimensional eigenspaces each carrying one copy of Γ; restricting the left action to one gives Γ. The gauge (which eigenspace, which phases) is a free choice, seeded and then **verified** — unitary, a homomorphism, and carrying the irrep's character, vectorised so the 96-element Oh double group costs 2 s. One trap: the generic element needs **both** Hermitian parts, `(C+C†)/2` *and* `i(C−C†)/2`; with only the first, C2v's E1/2 draw came out proportional to the identity and the commutant failed to split.

  **(ii) D^J from a quaternion** (`wigner_from_quaternion`), `exp(−iθ n·J)` by eigendecomposing the Hermitian `n·J`, which is exact and works for half-integer J — the quaternion carries θ ∈ [0, 2π] so ±q give different matrices, which is the whole spinor distinction. **Phase convention (recorded because it is invisible in the characters):** the exponential map and `point_group.wigner_D_matrix` differ by `D_package = Δ D_exp Δ^-1` with `Δ = diag(exp(iπm))` — measured at 1e-15 over all 48 Oh elements for J = 1..6, while the *characters are identical to 5.7e-15*. Untransformed, every subduction built here would have been silently incomparable with the existing Oh path. For integer J, Δ^-1 = Δ; for half-integer J, Δ² = −I, so it must be the similarity `Δ D Δ^-1` and **not** the sandwich `Δ D Δ`, which is not a representation at all.

  Oracles (`tests/test_angular/test_finite_group.py`, 209 tests, 3 s): isotypic projectors — gauge-free, so the right thing to compare — against the **production** `_real_subduction_matrix` for Oh at **8.0e-15** (J = 0..10, every irrep), `_complex_subduction_matrix_half_int` for the Oh double group at **1.1e-15** (J = 1/2..11/2, G3/2 included), and `d4h_partner_basis_per_J` for D4h at **2.0e-15** (J = 0..8 at the natural parity (−1)^J). Those three are the Fortran-validated path, so matching them means the direct O(3)→G projection needs no Oh→D4h chain. Plus, for the groups with no hand-written counterpart (Td, C4v, D3d, D2h, C2v): `S† D(g) S` equals the direct sum of the irrep matrices for **every** element, which pins multiplicities, partner order and gauge at once, and agreement with the gauge-free character projector, which involves no irrep matrices at all.

- **B3. Symmetry-adapted crystal field — DONE (2026-10-19, `angular/crystal_field.py`).** The identity that makes it short: `rac_generator._build_coupling_operator(J_b, J_k, k, a)` already *is* the Wybourne sum `sum_q a[q] T^(k)_q`, so an "operator vector" in this package is a row of B_kq. A tensor operator transforms as `U(g) T^(k)_q U(g)^dag = sum_q' D^(k)_{q'q}(g) T^(k)_{q'}`, so invariance is `D^(k)(g) B_k = B_k`, i.e. **B_k lies in the totally symmetric subspace of D^(k) restricted to G — which B2's `subduction(k, +1)` already returns**. Nothing else was needed for the allowed combinations, and nothing is tabulated.

  The free-parameter count therefore comes out of the group rather than a table, and it lands on the conventional value every time: **1 for Oh/O/Td/T (10Dq), 3 for D4h/D4/C4v/D2d and D3d/D3/C3v, 5 for D2h/D2/C2v.**

  **Naming a parameter needs one more input, and it is a convention rather than group theory.** The projection fixes no basis inside a multi-dimensional invariant space, so "Dt" is meaningless until the one-electron d energies it is defined by are supplied. Requiring the operator's one-electron matrix to equal `sqrt(2l+1)` times Ballhausen's pattern determines the vector uniquely — **direction, scale and sign together** — as a small linear solve (`cf_operator_from_pattern`). That is why **both hand-placed gauge pins of the old route are gone rather than ported**: the per-route `m = 0` positivity fix, which is residual 21 and without which a flipped LAPACK eigenvector sign mixed the cubic field into Dt by up to 1.6 eV, and the overall Ballhausen sign pin on the z^2 element. The `sqrt(2l+1)` is the module's one constant and it was *measured*: all three hand-coded D4h operators carry exactly that factor relative to their Ballhausen patterns, residuals <= 5e-15 and no off-diagonal part at all.

  **The pattern solve had to move into the complex basis, and my own gauge test did not catch why (worth remembering).** The first version built the least-squares problem from `_operator_real_matrix`, which keeps only `Re(U O U^dag)`. That throws away exactly what makes the solve gauge-independent: multiply an invariant basis vector by a complex phase and the real part no longer determines it. It looked right because the invariant basis comes out of `eigh` **essentially real when left alone** (measured max imaginary part 4.4e-16), and because the gauge test I wrote rotated the basis with a *real* 2x2 rotation, which a real-extracted solve survives.
