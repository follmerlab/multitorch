## The five new findings, worst first

### 1. `sym='td'` is a silent no-op (residual 73)

`calcXAS_from_scratch(..., sym='td', cf={'tendq': t})` returns the **same
spectrum as `sym='oh'`, bit for bit** — measured max|Δy| = **0.000e+00** at
`t = +1.0` and again at `t = −4/9`. That is the exact-equality construction td
was landed on, seen from the user's side. What was never stated is the
consequence: `cos(td(+1.0), td(−4/9)) = 0.688`. A user who types the positive
10Dq they would naturally write down for a tetrahedral complex gets **the
octahedral spectrum**, not a slightly wrong one, and nothing in the call says
so. The negative-sign requirement is in the docstring and in nothing else.

### 2. The energy axis has three zeros for one ion (residual 74)

Ni(II), bright sticks, measured today: `calcXAS(…,'oh','l')` **14.5–36.9 eV**,
`calcXAS(…,'d4h','l')` **855.2–879.4 eV**, `calcXAS_from_scratch(…, sym='oh')`
**−4.8–15.2 eV**. The 840 eV gap is which fixture was bundled, not physics —
`nid8ct` carries the Fortran E_av and `ni2_d8_oh` does not; from scratch there
is no absolute scale, because F⁰ is dropped on both sides. `calcXAS`'s
`Returns` says only "Energy axis (eV)". It *is* documented — in
`fixture_defaults.__doc__`, a function the user never has to call.

### 3. D4h and D3d from-scratch pay full angular cost on every call (residual 75)

Second call in a warm process: oh Fe(II) **0.14 s**, d4h Fe(II) **4.71 s**,
d3d Fe(II) **2.26 s**. WP-0's memoization reached Oh and not the other two.
`cProfile` names it: `symmetry.oh_to_group_subduction_matrix` called **1507
times for ~10 distinct argument pairs**, 8.53 s cumulative, of which
**453,170 `np.allclose` calls for 6.76 s — about 77 % of the call is
self-verification**, re-run identically every time. `point_group.py` already
`@lru_cache`s eleven functions of exactly this shape.

### 4. Three validation-surface gaps (residual 76)

(a) `test_deployment_checks.py:120` **skips** when no sticks are produced, on a
deterministic fixture — so a regression that empties the stick list turns the
dipole sum-rule test green. (b) `NUMBER_AUDIT.md` has **one entry**, and
SCA-001 just moved the nine Thompson (1987) coefficients onto the **default**
path with no ledger entry. (c) `ni2_oh_sc_hamiltonian.npz` has good in-file
provenance but no regeneration script, unlike `oh8`.

### 5. `calc.py`'s duplication has already cost three real defects (residual 77)

`CODE_REVIEW_2026-09.md` §D named "duplicated pipelines" and nothing was
extracted. Today: **4 copies of the stick → window → broaden → return tail**,
of which `diff` says three differ **only in the error string and two
comments**, and **3 copies of the fixture build preamble** — plus a fifth copy
of the cache route in `analysis/ground_state.py:155`, which imports
`_cache_ban` and `_check_atomic`, so a lower layer depends on the API layer's
privates. `_calcXAS_phase5` *is* `_run_phase5_pipeline` + the tail, verbatim.

What that has already cost, each verified rather than reasoned about:

- **`atomic` overrides cannot reach `calcXAS`.** `build_cowan_store_in_memory`
  accepts them; `calcXAS_cached` and `calcXAS_from_scratch` expose them;
  `calcXAS` has no such parameter and `_calcXAS_phase5` never passes one.
- **`device='auto'` works on `calcXAS_cached` and raises a raw PyTorch
  `RuntimeError` on `calcXAS`** — the sentinel is resolved per entry point, four
  inconsistent ways. (`device=None` is fine on both; I checked, because the
  first reading of this said otherwise.)
- **One of the four tails is chained to no equivalence test.** Three are tied
  together by `test_fixture_cache.py` and `test_batch_api.py`; the
  `_calcXAS_from_ban` tail is not.

Also: **`calcXES` and `getXES` have zero test references.**

## Lessons

1. **An audit document is a claim about a commit, and it keeps claiming it.**
   Two root-level audits still read "Trust level: LOW" for regimes that were
   fixed months ago. A verdict with no expiry date and no commit in its title
   is a liability pointing the wrong way, and it was found by this audit
   reading its own predecessor, not by anyone reading the code.
2. **The cheapest findings were in the one thing nobody re-reads: the public
   signature.** Three API defects — td's unguarded sign, three energy zeros,
   case-sensitive `sym` on the entry point the docs recommend — took one
   `inspect.signature` call and three spectra. None of them is in the physics,
   and all of them produce a wrong answer for a user who did nothing wrong.
3. **"Documented" is not the same as "where the user will look."** The energy
   zero *is* written down, in the docstring of a helper nobody has to call. The
   td sign *is* written down, in the from-scratch docstring, with no guard. Both
   facts were discoverable; neither was placed where the mistake happens.
4. **A profile of the second call says something a profile of the first cannot.**
   Oh and D4h look alike on a cold process (0.98 s vs 4.72 s) and are a factor
   of 34 apart on a warm one, because one path's constants are cached and the
   other's are not. The measurement that mattered was running the same call
   twice.
5. **Check whether a prior recommendation list was implemented before writing a
   new one.** Seven of the 2026-09 audit's eight were done, several with new
   Fortran oracles. Re-deriving them would have buried the one that was not
   (`pytest.skip` → fail) in a list of eight things already fixed.
