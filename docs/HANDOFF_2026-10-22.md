## What landed

### Residual 58 — the empty operator block (closes residual 44)

`ttrac.c`'s `execute_racer_task` (lines 7087–7137) writes the `REDUCEDMATRIX`
header and the matching `END` **unconditionally** for every present triad whose
operator irrep matches the actor; the `ADD` lines are whatever the inner triad
loop finds, possibly none. The discriminating fixture is
`tests/reference_data/ti4_d0_oh/ti4_d0_oh.rme_rac` **section 3** — a
data-carrying section, not one of the leading PAIRIN placeholder sections —
where the 3d⁰ ground manifold has no crystal field and its `10DQ` block is
emitted empty immediately after a `HAMILTONIAN` block on the same irrep that
carries a real ADD.

So **Oh was faithful and D4h's skip was the deviation.** Measured before
changing anything: removing the skip adds **6 blocks at exactly one case** of
the 27 (`d4h` d9 — `10DQ`, `DT`, `DS` on both `1/2-` and `3/2-`, all empty),
removes none, and leaves every pre-existing block unchanged and in the same
order. Residual 44 said "two empty `10DQ` blocks", which was the Oh side's
count; the D4h side restores three operators on two irreps.

The legacy emission moved with the plan emission — both had to, or B5-3's
byte-equality harness would merely have reported the difference being
introduced.

### Residual 59 — the general Oh→G subduction

`oh_to_group_subduction_matrix(oh_irrep, target_group)` is
`oh_to_d4h_subduction_matrix` with the D4-specific pieces taken from
`point_group(H)`: the rotation filter (matrix membership, not
`_is_d4z_rotation`), the classes and characters (not `_classify_d4_rotation`
and `D4_CHARACTERS`), and the branching (the character sum, not `OH_TO_D4H`).
The 2-D irrep stays geometric — a dihedral E is the action on the plane
perpendicular to the principal axis, which **is** `R[:2, :2]` in the z setting,
so no second rotation convention enters (residual 47).

**Gate: exact equality on all ten Oh single-group irreps, max difference 0.0.**
`oh_to_target_subduction_matrix` routes D4h through it and the tetragonal
function is retained as its oracle, not deleted (residual 45: the equality is
evidence only while the two are genuinely two implementations).

Two things the measurement caught that reading would not have:

- `point_group`'s character table is **computed**, so a character that is
  exactly ±1 arrives as `1 − 2.2e-16`. Left in, that noise reaches the partner
  vectors and the agreement is 3.3e-16 rather than 0.0 — correct, but a
  recomputation, and it would have shifted every published D4h coefficient in
  its last decimal. `_denoise_character` snaps only near-integer values, and a
  test removing it asserts the difference is non-zero and below 1e-14, so it
  pins which regime the gate is in.
- The first draft summed over `point_group('D4h')` rather than its rotation
  subgroup `D4`, and emitted keys like `A1gg`. The projector runs over **proper
  rotations only**, because the parity half of the subduction is already fixed
  by the Oh irrep's own g/u.

Independent checks, not code-vs-code: the partner bases block-diagonalise the
group for every rotation, both targets, all ten irreps; and the Oh→D3d
branching composed with `oh_branching` reproduces `point_group('D3d').branching`
for J = 0..4 and both parities by a route that never mentions Oh. `D3d[z]` is
refused with "is it registered in the cubic setting?" — residual 40's trap as an
error message rather than a wrong basis.

The block-diagonalisation oracle passed on the first run, which the trap list
says is when to doubt it, so three bugs were injected: swapped partner columns
(the 2026-09 S4 audit's own failure mode) caught in 10 of 20 cases — only where
an irrep has more than one column, which is the right answer rather than a weak
one — the raw Oh basis in 12 of 20, a dropped normalisation in 20 of 20, the
real function in 0 of 20.

### Residual 60 — Butler's `O=>D3`, and why step 2 is not a relabelling

`_d4_double_irrep_matrices` does not construct D4's double irreps; it takes
them **whole**, as restrictions of Oh's (E1/2 = Γ6↓ to the 8 D4_z rotations,
E3/2 = Γ7↓, both irreducible there). For D3 that is unavailable: Γ6↓ and Γ7↓
are irreducible but **equivalent to each other**, so they give one irrep rather
than two, and D3's two spinor irreps of dimension **one** occur nowhere but
inside Γ8↓, which splits `[2, 1, 1]`.

Butler's table in the tree says exactly this. `ttmult/src/disk0`, block
`$F3/OD3`, header "BRANCHDATA O=>D3 CREATED FOR_BUTLER(1981) 28_MAR_1980":

```
BRANCHINGS 0 +0 ; S0 +S0 ; 1 +^0 +1 ; S1 +S0 +S1 +-S1 ;
           2 +1 ; ^1 +0 +1 ; ^S0 +S0 ; ^0 +^0 ;
```

Its single-group rows — `T1 → A2 + E`, `T2 → A1 + E`, `E → E` — confirm residual
59's descent against the table **ttrac itself reads** rather than against
`point_group`, and `Γ8 → E1/2 + S1 + (−S1)` names the two 1-D irreps as the
conjugate pair they are.

Landed: the table transcribed with provenance; a composition oracle (SO3→Oh
branching followed by Butler's table reproduces
`point_group('D3', spinor=True).branching` for J = 0..11/2, all twelve, by two
routes neither derived from the other); the restriction split with an
irreducibility check per block; a drift guard re-reading the `BRANCHINGS` line
from `disk0` through the script's own parser and asserting its gate first; and
an injection swapping T2's row for T1's that requires J = 2 to notice.

### Residual 61 — what Butler fixes about the 1-D pair, and what he does not

The two 1-D blocks inside Γ8↓D3 differ in **exactly one** quantity: the sign of
the C2 eigenvalue, `+i` against `−i`. Both have C3 = −1, which is not what a
complex-conjugate pair suggests, and the real puzzle was how `S1 ⊗ S1 → ^0`
could hold at all. It holds because the discriminating character is the C2 one:
`(±i)² = −1` is A2's C2 character, matching `S1 S1 ^0 0 + CHOSEN`, and
`(+i)(−i) = +1` is A1's, matching `-S1 S1 0 0 -`. Both come out Butler's way
with nothing fitted, which independently corroborates the extraction.

Fixing the **absolute** assignment would need Butler's spinor *lift* of the C2
elements, which is 2jm/3jm data, not a readable line. And because the pair is
Kramers-degenerate, swapping the labels conjugates the Hamiltonian block and
leaves every eigenvalue alone.

**So the choice is ours to state, the rule is clean — the sign of the C2
eigenvalue — and the thing to test is invariance under swapping it in both
manifolds together against a change when only one is swapped.** Do not chase
agreement with Butler on which is which.

The bug the question exposed: `parse_irreps` in `scripts/derive_butler_routes.py`
read Butler's IRREPS line two tokens at a time, which is the natural thing to
do. A conjugate pair is written `X * -X <dim>`, with the marker in the **first**
member's dimension slot and the shared dimension after the **second**, so
reading in twos drops the first member of every pair — `S1` for D3 and D5, `S1`
and `2` for T, and one or more for every cyclic group. **Latent, not live:**
`ButlerTables.irreps` is populated and never read, and the script's gate covers
only Oh and D4h, which have no pairs — so the first consumer would have been the
trigonal double group. Fixed, with `sum(dim²) == |double group|` as the oracle:
the old reader puts D3 at **11 instead of 12** and gets 6 of the 8 tabulated
groups wrong while leaving O at 48 and D4 at 16, which measures why it was
invisible.

### Residual 62 — no dim-2 assumption; the lookup was the gate

Every downstream consumer of a double-irrep dimension takes it as a
**variable**: `sqrt(dim / (2J + 1))` in the operator coefficient,
`sub[:, copy * dim:(copy + 1) * dim]` in `GaugedBasis.target_partners`,
`range(dim)` and a multiplicity check in `d4h_double_layout`, the dim-table
selection in `_d4h_manifold_irreps`. Not one hardcodes 2.

`target_double_irrep_dim` now falls through to `point_group(G, spinor=True)` and
returns D3d's `{E1/2: 2, 1E3/2: 1, 2E3/2: 1}`, with Oh and D4h keeping the
tables the Fortran parity runs through.

The probe: forcing the lookup to 1 and regenerating d7 (half-integer J in both
manifolds, so every block is a double-group one) scales **every** operator
coefficient by exactly `sqrt(1/2)`, one ratio value across HAMILTONIAN, 10DQ,
DT and DS over 500+ coefficients.

## What is left: the trigonal chain, two pieces not three

### 1. The D3d double subduction, with its Γ provider

The method is verified; what is left is writing it.

- **Per-irrep source rule:** take each target double irrep from a fixed Oh
  source — the **whole restriction** where it is irreducible, which is what
  preserves D4h bit for bit, and **isotypic extraction from Γ8** otherwise.
- **The gate:** bit-exact against `oh_to_d4h_double_subduction`, the same
  discipline residual 59 used. D4h's realization is pinned by Fortran parity
  and must not be re-gauged — in particular do **not** reach for
  `point_group(G, spinor=True).irrep_matrices`, whose gauge is a seeded `eigh`
  and would move D4h's partner bases by more than a phase.
- **`S1` pinned by the sign of the C2 eigenvalue**, documented as a convention,
  tested by swap-invariance across both manifolds rather than by agreement with
  Butler.
- The plumbing is ready: `target_double_irrep_dim` knows the dimensions and the
  arithmetic is dimension-general (residual 62).

### 2. The route phases

Dσ and Dτ are D3d scalars but **not** Oh scalars, exactly as DT/DS are D4h
scalars but not Oh ones, so the couplings come out complex until one phase per
(Oh irrep → D3d irrep) route is pinned. `_pin_d4h_routes` is the pattern.
**This one has no bit-exactness gate available** — there is nothing existing to
reproduce — which makes it the hardest remaining piece and the one most in need
of an oracle designed before code.

## Lessons from this session

1. **Before building an oracle, ask what the Fortran does — the tidier
   behaviour is not the question.** Residual 44 sat open for three days behind a
   prescribed one-liner that pointed the wrong way, and the answer was a grep
   and one fixture away the whole time.
2. **A decomposition can land on the right block structure out of arithmetic
   that does not commute.** The first commutant solve used the column-major vec
   identity — the natural thing to write, wrong for numpy's row-major
   `reshape`. It returned matrices commuting with *nothing* (max |XD − DX| ≈
   0.7) and still split Γ8 as `[1,1,2]`, exactly what Butler says, both 1-D
   blocks at the right dimension. Only the irreducibility norm reading 0.333
   instead of 1.0 gave it away. **Assert the invariance, not the shapes.**
3. **Before generalising a function, check whether it constructs the thing or
   just takes it.** `_d4_double_irrep_matrices` reads like a constructor and is
   a restriction, which is why "make it general" was a short job for the
   integer case and is not one for the half-integer case.
4. **A computed table is not a tabulated one.** `point_group`'s characters
   carry ~2e-16 noise where hand-written literals are exact; that difference is
   invisible until it decides whether a switch is a relabelling or a
   recomputation.
5. **A scoping list written from reading is a hypothesis.** Three of this
   session's five increments contradicted one. Measure the item before
   committing to the shape of the work — and when a sub-problem is named after
   a concept rather than a function, grep for the function before believing it
   exists.
