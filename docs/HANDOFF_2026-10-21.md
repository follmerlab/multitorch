## The one thing that is genuinely still open: D3d from `calcXAS`

D3d now has **correct, published, provenance-pinned crystal-field conventions**
(`ONE_ELECTRON_PATTERNS['D3d']`) and a route-resolved descent limit to Oh. It is
still **not reachable from `calcXAS`**, and what stands in the way is
engineering rather than physics or names. Scoped, in dependency order:

1. **A general Oh→G subduction.** `symmetry.oh_to_target_subduction_matrix`
   currently handles only the Oh identity and D4h; the D4h case is
   `oh_to_d4h_subduction_matrix`, which builds character projectors over the 8
   D4_z rotations inside Oh. **That construction generalises directly** — swap
   `_is_d4z_rotation` for membership of the target group's rotation set and the
   D4 classes for the target's own, both available from `point_group(G)`. This
   is the main piece.
2. **The double-group counterpart**, `oh_to_d4h_double_subduction` →
   general. Needed for odd electron counts.
3. **A residual-31 analogue.** Dσ and Dτ are D3d scalars but **not** Oh
   scalars, exactly as DT/DS are D4h scalars but not Oh ones, so the couplings
   will come out complex until one phase per (Oh irrep → D3d irrep) subduction
   route is pinned. `_pin_d4h_routes` is the pattern; expect to need its
   trigonal twin, and expect it to be the hard part.
4. **`_d3d_manifold_irreps` / layout**, the counterparts of
   `_d4h_manifold_irreps` and `d4h_basis_layout`.
5. **`symmetry_plan.CONVENTIONS['D3d']`** — store strings and public keys for
   `(10DQ, DSIGMA, DTAU)`, three hybridisation channels (A1g, Eg×2), and two
   dipole Butler labels. **The Butler labels are already derived: `^0-` and
   `1-`, the same strings D4h uses**, because `O=>D3` branches Oh T1u to A2 + E
   just as `O=>D4` does. `GEOMETRY_NAMES['D3d']` likewise follows from
   `polarization_axis`, which has worked off-axis since B5-8.
6. **The dipole emitter**, and residual 57 says which sign rule to give it:
   **the Oh rule** (one globally-ranked partner triple per block), because it is
   the one chosen deliberately and the one that generalises to irreps of
   dimension 3.

**Oracle for the whole chain:** the descent limit. At zero distortion D3d's
crystal-field operator is *the same array* as Oh's (residual 51, ≤ 1.8e-15), so
a from-scratch D3d spectrum at Dσ = Dτ = 0 must equal the Oh one bit for bit,
and Oh is Fortran-validated. Away from that limit the Kaizaki one-electron
energies are the analytic oracle, the trigonal counterpart of what
`test_cf_one_electron.py` already runs for D4h.

## B5-5b: three steps landed, the fourth declined on measurement

Each step was **measured before being implemented**, which is residual 48's
lesson, and it changed what each increment was. The plan warned that routing Oh
through the dispatcher "changes which code computes the coefficients, so byte
equality becomes the claim rather than the check". For the operator emitters
that worry does not apply, and the measurements say why:

| step | path | measured |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | integer operator | 80 cases, 608 ADD entries, **max difference 0.0** |
| 3 | half-integer operator | 54 cases, 868 ADD entries, **max difference 0.0** |
| 4 | dipole | **not** a relabelling — see below |

So `_make_op_adds` and `_make_op_adds_half` now serve Oh and D4h, with
`target_group=` selecting the subduction and `rank=` coming from the group's
`SymmetryPlan` instead of a table of the three D4h names.

**The retired Oh emitters are retained, not deleted.** They are the only
independent construction of these coefficients, which is what licenses the
switch, so deleting them would delete the oracle (residual 38).
`test_the_retired_oh_operator_emitter_is_off_the_production_path` holds the
position from both sides: zero calls from the plan emission *and* from
`ct_generator`, more than zero from the legacy emission.

### Step 4, and why not to do it

The two dipole emitters use **different sign rules**: Oh fixes one partner
triple per (Γ_gs, Γ_ex) block (largest summed |me|), D4h takes the first
non-zero element at p_gs = 0. `_oh_dipole_pair_elements`'s comment says the
second is the rule it refused, because it "could switch triples between pairs".

Measured, and **both of my readings were wrong**:

- That worry **never materialises**: zero blocks out of 52 (D4h) and zero out of
  100 (Oh) in which the first-nonzero rule picks more than one triple. **The
  shipped D4h dipole is sound and the comment overstates the risk.**
- What the rules differ in is **which** triple they pick, hence a sign: 245 of
  460 pairs for Oh, 191 of 1286 for D4h. But the sign *ratio* is uniform within
  every block — 48 of 48 for Oh, 200 of 200 for D4h, **zero** varying inside —
  so the two differ by at most a **global sign per block**, never a relative
  sign within one, and that cannot change an intensity while Oh irreps do not
  mix.

Neither rule is a no-op for the other group, so unifying in either direction
changes the sign convention of a Fortran-validated path **for no capability
gain** — B5-5b is cleanup, and after B5-9 the loop is general without it. If a
shared dipole emitter is wanted later, make the sign rule a **parameter of the
plan** so both validated paths keep their own.

## Conventions (unchanged, and all of them earned their keep again)

- Commit to `develop` after each increment with a green suite; stage files
  explicitly. **Ask before pushing. Do not merge to `main` without asking.**
- Every physics change gets a Fortran, analytic or finite-difference oracle —
  **never code-vs-code**. An external, independently validated source is a
  legitimate oracle class, below Fortran/analytic, and its provenance goes in
  **`NUMBER_AUDIT.md`**.
- **A new oracle is not an oracle until you inject the bug it is meant to catch
  and watch it fail.** It caught a tautological test of mine this session.
- Tests: `/opt/anaconda3/envs/multi/bin/pytest tests/ -q -p no:cacheprovider`

- **Restore an injected bug from a file backup, not `git checkout`.**
- `pkill -f <pattern>` self-matches over ssh; use `pgrep -f "foo[.]py"`.

### Lessons that cost something this session

1. **A number you could measure should never be predicted.** Three times:
   I claimed the 3×3 map from (10Dq, Dσ, Dτ) to the three diagonal energies
   inverts — it is rank 2, because the field is traceless; I guessed
   per-operator case counts (26/182, 26/686) that were 27/131 and 25/737; and
   both my readings of the dipole sign rules were wrong. Each was caught
   immediately by an assertion, which is the discipline working — but running it
   first is cheaper than being corrected.
2. **Before renaming a shared table, grep what each consumer *guards on*.**
   Renaming `ct_generator.CF_ACTORS`'s Oh row to the convention name fed
   `'TENDQ'` to an emitter guarding on `!= '10DQ'`; every odd-electron CT ion
   failed. The fix was to remove the ambiguity — one name per thing — not to
   patch the call site.
3. **A test can be a tautology and pass at 1.000000000000000.** "The A1g route
   is the cubic invariant" held for all 14 groups *by construction*, since the
   image of `P_A1g` on D^(4) **is** that line whatever subgroup is on the other
   side. Found by injection, not by reading. Each surviving rung's blind spot is
   now recorded in its own docstring.
4. **A plausible reading of two functions is not a measurement of them.**
   Steps 1–3 were relabellings and step 4 was not; no amount of reading
   separated those cases, and in step 3 the reading also missed a *demanded*
   route phase that Oh never has and that would have made every Oh half-integer
   call raise.
5. **Check the machine before believing a regression.** A suite that read
   29:42 against 11:36 was load average 57 from an unrelated process; the
   generator benchmarked at 1174 ms against 1178 ms at HEAD.
