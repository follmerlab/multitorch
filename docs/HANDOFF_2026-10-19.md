### Lessons that cost something to learn

Read these before writing a test.

1. **Grep for call sites before trusting any function the plan names**
   (residual 41). Three functions the plan listed as work targets were already
   dead: `oh_subduction_matrix` (B2's named *oracle*, and numerically wrong),
   `d4h_cf_operator_recipe` (B3's target), `_make_operator_adds` /
   `_make_cf_adds` (B5's). The plan's line references decay too — B5's
   `assemble.py:246-253` pointed at a docstring. Grepping first cost nothing in
   B3/B4/B5; skipping it cost a wrong conclusion once, in B2.
2. **A basis-choice test must scramble the way the harness does — complex
   phases, not just rotations.** B3's first pattern solve used the real part
   only. It looked right because `eigh` returns an essentially real basis when
   left alone (max imaginary part 4.4e-16) *and* because the gauge test used a
   **real** rotation. The LAPACK scramble harness caught it: at a phase of
   `exp(0.7i)` the answer was wrong by **21 in a vector of norm 25 while the
   solve's own residual stayed at 5.3e-15** — silently wrong, not caught. Solve
   in the complex basis.
3. **Align cross-group spectra on a *bright* stick, never on `E.min()`**
   (residual 36). D4h lists dark ground-final pairs Oh does not have; aligning
   on the extremum gave a fake cosine of 0.10–0.60 and sent me chasing a
   non-existent bug.
4. **A property asserted only over the target list is not asserted.**
   `test_first_irrep_is_totally_symmetric` ran over the 13 WP-B targets, so
   nobody noticed it was false for **T**. B3 found it by widening the group set.
5. **A plausible physical ratio is not an oracle** (residual 39). L3/L2 looked
   like a correctness check until the same measurement on the eight
   Fortran-validated fixtures spanned 0.55–4.98.
6. **When a test fails on a NEW path, run it on the known-good OLD path before
   believing the new code is broken.** That is what turned finding 5 from "the
   engine is broken" into "the acceptance band is wrong".
7. Comparing level lists across two point groups needs degeneracy weighting
   (`_levels_by_state`). Vary one term at a time; a presence test proves almost
   nothing.
8. **Do not compare a d⁵ absolute intensity across 2026-10-18** without
   checking the Boltzmann pool (residual 37).

### Task 3 — WP-B5, the remaining headline work

**Work the B5-3 … B5-9 table in the plan's WP-B5 entry in order.** This is a
deliberate decision (user, 2026-10-19): B5-9 is the only increment that makes a
new group reachable from `calcXAS`, and it would be reachable sooner by doing
B5-3 and then jumping to B5-9 — but that leaves the branchy generator in place.
**The visible win is deferred on purpose; do not reorder to chase it.**

In brief:

- **B5-3** keep today's generator as `_generate_ledge_template_legacy` and build
  the fingerprint-equality harness over the 18 (sym, n) cases. Green trivially
  at first — that is the point; it proves the net before anything moves. Use
  `multitorch/io/rac_text.py` (`rac_fingerprint`, `rac_digest`,
  `rac_fingerprint_diff`), added by B5-1 for exactly this. **Do not store golden
  files** — full fingerprints for the 18 cases are 1.35 MiB; compare against the
  retained legacy function in-process, the way B3 kept the Butler route.
- **B5-4** generator returns its own operator list and hybridisation channels.
- **B5-5 / B5-6** collapse the two parallel emission paths, GROUND/EXCITE first,
  then TRANSI. The generator is `if sym == 'd4h'` blocks at `rac_generator.py`
  1867/1951/1981/2012/2115 plus three
  `legacy_*_irreps = oh_irreps_* if sym == 'oh' else []` loops at 2063/2208/2256
  — which is why the work splits by block kind.
- **B5-7** retire the Butler-string geometry match at `assemble.py:625`.
- **B5-8 settle residual 40, the trigonal setting.** `GENERATORS` puts every
  principal axis along z, which is right for tetragonal and orthorhombic (D4h,
  D4, C4v, D2d, D2h, D2, C2v are genuine Oh subgroups and contain the cubic
  invariant to ≤6.5e-16) and **wrong for trigonal**: with C3 ∥ z, 8 of D3d's 12
  elements fall outside Oh and the cubic invariant is not in their rank-4 space
  (residual 0.6455). Rebuilt with C3 ∥ [111] and C2 ∥ (1,−1,0), D3d *is* an Oh
  subgroup and it drops to 9.4e-16. `PRINCIPAL_AXIS` in `finite_group.py` exists
  for this and is still empty. Blocks B5-9 for D3d/D3/C3v — three of the five
  target groups.
- **B5-9 add Td end-to-end** — the cheapest third group (one cubic CF parameter
  and one dipole irrep, exactly like Oh). The first increment that makes a new
  group reachable from `calcXAS`.

Do **not** invent named CF conventions for groups with no Fortran oracle. B3
deliberately left `(10Dq, Dσ, Dτ)` for D3d/C3v and the five-parameter D2h/C2v
sets unnamed: those need a literature reference, and guessing plausible numbers
is residual 39's trap. Any new group is already usable through
`crystal_field.cf_invariant_vectors` / `cf_operator_from_pattern` and
`operator_subduction.operator_components` — only the *naming* is missing.
